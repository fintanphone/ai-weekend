Lab 4 · Retrieval

What the model actually sees

Retrieval-augmented generation has an intimidating name and a mundane mechanism. Here is the literal text that goes in, and the text that comes back.

Nothing is added to the model. No weights change, no training happens, nothing is remembered.

Your code looks up some text, pastes it above the question, and sends the whole thing as one ordinary prompt. That is the entire technique.

The pipeline

Four stages, and only one of them is interesting

Stages one and two happen once, before anyone asks anything. Stages three and four happen per question. The lab's corpus is five short HR policy documents from a fictional freight company.

01 · once Chunk Split each document into retrievable pieces. Here, on paragraph breaks. → 20 chunks
02 · once Embed Turn each chunk into a vector that encodes roughly what it is about. → 20 × 384 floats
03 · per question Retrieve Embed the question too, then find the chunks whose vectors point the same way. → top 3
04 · per question Generate Paste those chunks into a prompt alongside the question. Send it. → ~450 tokens

Stage three decides what the model can know. Stage four only decides how well it says it. When an answer comes back wrong, almost everyone debugs stage four.

scores = index @ qvec              # cosine similarity
top    = np.argsort(scores)[::-1][:k]

The entire "vector database", as it appears in rag.py. Everything else in the retrieval tooling ecosystem is operational convenience wrapped around those two lines.

The output

An ordinary completion

You accrue 25 days of annual leave per year, accruing monthly from your start date. Public holidays are additional and are not deducted from that allowance. Note that leave does not carry over into the following calendar year without written approval from a director.

Correct, and grounded — every claim traces back to orange text. But the model did not know any of it. Ask the same question with the retrieval step switched off and you get a confident invention, because nothing in the prompt would have contradicted it.

Misconceptions

What did not happen

  • The model was not trained, fine-tuned or updated. Its weights are identical before and after. Ask the same question tomorrow with the retrieval step removed and it knows nothing.
  • The documents were not "loaded into" the model. Three paragraphs were pasted into one prompt. The other seventeen chunks were never sent and the model has no idea they exist.
  • Nothing was remembered. The API is stateless. The next question re-runs retrieval from scratch and re-sends everything it needs.
  • No search engine was consulted. Similarity here is one matrix multiply against 20 vectors held in memory. At this scale a database would be slower.

Where it breaks

Three questions this cannot answer

Each of these fails structurally, not because the model is weak. Swapping in a bigger model changes nothing, because the right text never reached the prompt in the first place.

Multi-hop

caused at stage 01 · chunking

"What notice period does a Warehouse Supervisor have to give?"

The answer needs two facts from two documents — Supervisor is Senior Associate grade, and below-manager grade means one month's notice. No single chunk holds both, and neither scores well against the combined question. Plausible text arrives; the answer is wrong.

Negation

caused at stage 02 · embedding

"Which roles cannot work remotely?"

Embeddings encode subject matter, not polarity. "May work remotely" and "may not work remotely" sit almost on top of each other in vector space, because they are about the same thing. The exclusion clause does not distinguish itself.

Aggregation

caused at stage 03 · top-k

"How many policies mention a monetary limit?"

Top-k retrieval returns the three best matches. A question about the whole corpus needs all twenty. Raising k doesn't fix the category of problem — it moves the threshold and inflates every prompt you send.

The fix for the first one is worth finding yourself: answer "what grade is a Warehouse Supervisor?" first, then use that answer to make a second query. That is retrieval as a tool inside Lab 3's loop rather than a fixed pipeline step — and it is the whole difference between RAG and agentic RAG.
